How the engine calculates

The scan → opportunity pipeline, end to end. Each step is driven by the parameters in the registry below — change a dial and you change that step's behaviour on the next run.

  1. 1
    Rank the categories (scan)score = Prize × Feasibility × Provability²Every sub-category is scored on size (Prize), how actionable it is (Feasibility), and how defensible the savings are (Provability). Provability is squared so it dominates — we only chase savings we can prove. The top-ranked sub-categories go to deep-dive.Tuned by:prov_exponentfeas_frag_weightfeas_xbu_weightscan_deep_dive_top_n
  2. 2
    Define the pocketpocket = L3 category × countryA pocket is the unit we actually source — one L3 commodity in one country. A pocket must clear a minimum spend and vendor count to be worth acting on.Tuned by:play_min_spendplay_min_vendors
  3. 3
    Segment gate (engineered carve-out)engineered → OEM carve-out (no consolidation)Engineered items (e.g. 'Machine parts') are tied to their OEM and can't be competitively re-sourced, so they're carved out of the consolidation logic rather than forced into a play.Tuned by:engineered_segments
  4. 4
    Find the winner + route the playwinner_share = top non-OEM vendor ÷ pocket spend → ≥ 50% Consolidate · else RFPThe winner is the largest non-OEM supplier in the pocket. If they already hold at least half the spend, we fold the tail to them (Consolidate to incumbent). If it's fragmented, we contest the whole base (Competitive RFP).Tuned by:winner_share_consolidate
  5. 5
    Compute movable spendConsolidate → pocket − winner − OEM RFP → pocket − OEMMovable spend is what we can realistically move: the incumbent's own spend and OEM/sole-source spend are removed. This is the base the savings rate applies to — not the headline spend.Tuned by:engineered_segments
  6. 6
    Apply lever-tiered savingssavings = movable × rate (consolidate 4–7% · RFP 5–8% · fragmented tail 6–10% · services 5–8%)The savings rate depends on the lever — consolidation to an incumbent yields less than competing a fragmented base. Rates are conservative ranges, not point estimates.Tuned by:savings_rate.consolidate_incumbentsavings_rate.competitive_rfpsavings_rate.fragmented_tailsavings_rate.services_ratecard

Numeric dials are editable — saved to the parameter store and applied on the engine's next run. Structural parameters (OEM definition, engineered segments, savings-rate policy) are read-only.

ParameterValueWhat it controls
Feasibility: fragmentation weightfeas_frag_weight

weight

Share of a category's Feasibility score driven by how fragmented its supplier base is (more fragmentation = more room to consolidate). Pairs with the cross-division weight; the two sum to 1.0.
Feasibility: cross-division weightfeas_xbu_weight

weight

Share of a category's Feasibility score driven by overlapping AT + AOH demand (cross-business-unit leverage potential). Pairs with the fragmentation weight; the two sum to 1.0.
Provability weighting (exponent)prov_exponent

exponent

How heavily evidence strength dominates which categories rank first. The scan score is Prize x Feasibility x Provability^(this); a value of 2 squares provability, so well-evidenced categories rank far above speculative ones (the engagement is outcome-based).
Cross-BU normalization floorscan_xbu_norm_min_spend
$

USD

When scaling the cross-BU headroom (xbu) across sub-categories for the Feasibility score, only sub-categories with at least this much spend anchor the maximum. Prevents a tiny near-50/50 category (e.g. a $400k category split evenly across AT/AOH) from distorting the 0-1 scale and deflating everyone else.

factor

How much Provability is cut when the services trigger is exceeded. 0.60 means provability is multiplied by 0.6 (a 40% reduction), pushing services-heavy categories down the ranking.
