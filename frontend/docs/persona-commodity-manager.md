## Context: the merger made her job 3× bigger and her data 2× worse

Allison Transmission (AT) combined with Allison Off-Highway (AOH — EMEA-heavy, Italy-hub).
Combined indirect spend under review is **$957M** ($761.6M in-scope), served by **~8,000
cleaned vendor names** — and only **1.3% of vendors (107) serve both entities**, capturing
just $32.7M. The two companies source independently almost everywhere, so synergy is not
"merge the contracts": it's qualify-and-consolidate, category by category, country by country.

KPMG built the spend cube between January and April 2026 (project "Mamba" Phase 4) and
handed it off on April 14. The consultants are gone. The cube is a **decaying snapshot**
unless someone keeps working it — and that someone is Maria.

### The number that keeps her up at night

The company's 2029 synergy target is **$180M**, with $144.1M identified and a **$36M gap**.
Procurement carries 45% of identified value — but it's almost all direct commodities:

- **Commodities: $63.3M identified across 102 initiatives**
- **Indirect: $1.3M across 41 initiatives — 0 past Charter stage, 0 in execution**

Meanwhile the benchmark potential of her indirect estate is **$72.7M–$102.1M** (Hackett/
CAPS ranges applied per category; MRO alone is 13.2–18% ≈ **$14.4M–$19.7M**). The gap
between $1.3M booked and ~$87M possible is simultaneously her opportunity and the thing
she will be blamed for. Leadership expects a pipeline of **2× the target** for risk
adjustment; hers is nowhere close.

## Goals

1. **Convert white space into bankable savings** — pipeline coverage ≥2× her share of the
   synergy target, with evidence that survives an investment-committee review ("bankable,
   not a slide number").
2. **Rationalize the tail** — bring ~1,850 MRO vendors toward industry density; kill the
   $20K-average-spend vendor sprawl (e.g., 188 material-handling vendors at AT alone).
3. **Harmonize what the merger left misaligned** — payment terms ($274.7M of AT and
   $232.6M of AOH spend has *blank* terms data), pricing on shared vendors, taxonomy.
4. **Keep value from decaying** — committed plays must ramp and stay; re-fragmentation
   and onboarding stalls need to be caught early, not at year-end.

## KPIs she is measured on

- Year-over-year cost reduction / PPV against budget
- Identified vs. committed vs. **realized** savings (Shibumi stage progression)
- Pipeline coverage ratio (target ≥2×)
- Vendor count / tail-spend reduction per category
- Payment-terms harmonization (DPO uplift)
- Category turnover under management; geo/tariff risk exposure (post-Lens)

## Pain points

1. **Two ERPs, no single source of truth.** AT spend lives in Goods Receipt extracts;
   AOH in a lookup tool. The KPMG cube merged them once — refreshing it is manual.
2. **Classification debt.** Spend mapping is "95%+ complete" and was *driven by commodity
   manager inputs* — i.e., she validated mappings in 1–2 meetings per week for three
   months. The residual tail and every new month of data is on her now.
3. **AOH data quality blocks evidence.** Invoice quantities <2% populated, posting dates
   ~0%, payment terms blank — so unit-price comparisons are impossible cross-entity, and
   earlier savings claims had to be withdrawn. She is rightly skeptical of benchmark-only
   numbers.
4. **Pipeline sclerosis.** 28 of her 41 indirect initiatives sit at Idea, 13 at Charter,
   none in Analyze or Execution. Chartering takes meetings she doesn't have.
5. **Manual opportunity discovery.** Today an opportunity is found by a person staring at
   a pivot table. Headline category counts hide where the actionable concentration gaps
   are — the dollar moves live below L1, at L2 × country.
6. **Plant resistance.** Plant GMs fight regional bundles; MRO is plant-touching. She
   needs confidence numbers and evidence to win those fights.
7. **No vendor intelligence layer.** Lead-time changes, performance slips, and contract
   expiries live in inboxes. Vendor "scores" don't exist — only spend-shape metrics.

## Behaviors & attitudes

- **Evidence-first.** Trusts line-level Material Description review over benchmarks;
  expects the tool to show *casualties* (screened-out opportunities with reasons) — an
  honesty check that builds her trust in what survived.
- **Works highest-confidence first.** A small, clean opportunity beats a huge messy one.
  Confidence should be independent of dollar size.
- **Validates before committing.** Vendor-by-vendor checks against AT contract rates
  before any savings figure leaves her desk; finance sign-off is part of her definition
  of done.
- **Two play archetypes** in her head: *product consolidation* (compete two incumbent
  platforms — e.g., Fuchs the manufacturer vs. Lemak the distributor in US MRO chemicals)
  and *operating-model change* (e.g., migrate AT's 188 self-managed material-handling
  vendors onto AOH's Total Fleet Solutions managed-fleet contract — the 94× fragmentation
  gap play).
- Lives in Excel; will adopt a tool only if it's faster than her pivot tables on day one.

## What she needs from the product (Navanta Lens / this app)

1. **Scan → Qualify → Act → Monitor** as a working loop, not a consulting phase:
   AI scans every sub-category × country bucket, applies the two-gate screen
   (≥$250K both sides, ≥2× fragmentation gap), assigns fit and **confidence =
   fit × min(gap, 20)/20**, and surfaces a ranked feed.
2. **Review → commit/qualify/dismiss** on each opportunity, with the evidence attached
   (vendor rosters, fragmentation metrics, benchmark basis, data-reliability flags) and
   dismissals captured with reasons — feedback that tunes the engine.
3. **A vendor management surface**: categorized vendor roster with score, lead time,
   payment terms, entity origin, overlap flags — editable when reality changes (lead-time
   slip should take 10 seconds to record and should move the score).
4. **Value Realization tracking**: committed plays with stage, projected vs. realized
   ramp, milestones, owners — and **drift detection** that flags stalls before they slip.
5. **Caveat discipline built in**: data-reliability flags on every evidence row, savings
   labeled benchmark-basis vs. evidence-basis, scale-bias adjustments visible.

## Quote

> "Don't show me another 8% benchmark. Show me the eighteen vendors behind the number,
