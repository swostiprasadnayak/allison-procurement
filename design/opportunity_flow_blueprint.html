The Flow Map

The spine is real: Feed → Details → Approve → Act → Commit → Value Realization all exist and connect. The problems live in three places: the edges (Park and Reject exit into rooms with no working door back), the promises (five places where the UI announces behavior no code performs), and durability (decisions, notes, and audit events that silently vanish on reload). Solid blue = deployed and working. Dashed red = deployed but broken promise / dead end. Dashed green = build.

Stage 1 · Opportunity Feed — the triage queue

The CM's morning starts here. The job is throughput with confidence: separate the 2–3 items worth deep work from the noise, in minutes, without fear of missing something that changed overnight. Today's feed is a competent read-only table; it is not yet a triage instrument.

03

Stage 2 · Opportunity Details — the interrogation room

One click deep, the CM stops scanning and starts cross-examining: "$74.5K addressable in Chemicals · Mexico — says who?" This modal is the strongest screen in the build — summary, waterfall, fit checks, roster, caveats, override — but it argues from shares and rates, never from prices. For a skeptic under an outcome-fee model, that's the difference between plausible and proven.

Stage 3 · The Decision Moment — Approve / Park / Reject

The whole product funnels to three buttons. This is where trust is spent: the CM stakes their name on Approve, or tells the machine why not. The dialogs are well-designed; what's broken is what happens to the answer afterward — reasons the system asks for and then loses, and promises it makes and doesn't keep.

05

Stage 4 · Act — Run the Play

Approve moves the opp to the Act tab; clicking it opens the play workspace. The scaffolding is real — playbooks from the CDM, a task checklist, draft generators, a commit step that bakes the CM's overrides into the committed value. But as a place to run a live RFP, it stops at templates: nothing can be sent, compared, versioned, or fed back in. This is where the discovery's "evidence-based price & spec comparisons" must land.

06

Stage 5 · After Act — Commit → Value Realization

This stage exists and is better than expected — the adversarial pass refuted the "post-Act is a dead end" assumption. Commit hands off to /tracking: KPIs, a ramp chart of projected vs realized, per-play panels with a full activity trail, and a Mark-live stage toggle. What's missing is the ending: nothing ever measures realization, flags drift, or closes a play with a verdict. Under an outcome-based fee, the unfinished ending is the product.

09

Cross-Cutting Layers

Five threads that run through every stage rather than living on one screen.

① The pricing-evidence layer — from rates to prices

Today every savings number is rate-derived (a lever-tiered % of addressable). Nowhere — feed, details, act, tracking — does a unit price, benchmark, or price gap appear; the roster carries only aggregate spend and shares, and the one place named "Market intel / pricing data" is an empty box the CM is asked to fill. For a skeptical buyer, that's an argument without its strongest witness.

The bridge to build (Details modal + Act): benchmark price for the category/geography → ATI's own blended rate from transaction lines → gap per unit × addressable volume → the savings range. Per-vendor blended unit price in the roster; quote-vs-blended comparison when evidence lands in Act. This is literally the discovery's promise — "evidence-based price and spec comparisons on ATI's own transaction data."

② Ask Mercer — one placement, one behavior

Mercer is correctly scoped everywhere (the copilot opens with page + opportunity context, and the modal reserves 440px for the dock). Two fixes: anchor the button left of the decision cluster in every footer (today it drifts), and never dress non-AI output in Mercer's star — the Act drafts are deterministic templates wearing the copilot's badge, which is exactly the kind of over-claim a "prove it" buyer punishes.

③ Signals & notifications — the missing nervous system

All communication today is same-session toasts. Nothing announces: a new sweep landing, a parked trigger coming due, drift on a committed play, a new supplier entering a pocket, or a new BU's data arriving. One signal rail (feed chips + a brief line + a bell) serves every stage: New Changed +$41K New supplier Revisit due Drift — each chip answering "why am I seeing this now."

④ Truthful microcopy — say only what the code does

The audit found five places where copy promises behavior that doesn't exist: "Ranked by evidence strength" (sorts by confidence), "resurfaces when its revisit trigger fires" (nothing fires), "logged for sweep calibration" (engine reads nothing), the Mercer-starred template drafts, and a docstring's promised commit "Done" state. Each is small; together they train users to distrust labels — fatal under an outcome-based fee. Rule: every promise in copy maps to a code path, or the copy changes.

⑤ Durability — decisions that survive a refresh

Volatile today: qualify status, audit events, reject notes, CM context/market intel, figure overrides' provenance trail, drift state, demo actuals — plus save failures masked by success toasts and a store clock frozen at 2026-06-12. A finance-adjacent tool has one hard rule: what the user told the system, the system remembers — visibly, or it says why not.
